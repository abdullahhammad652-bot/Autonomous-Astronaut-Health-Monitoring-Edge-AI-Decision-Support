1. Executive Summary & Challenge Overview

During long-duration deep-space exploration missions (e.g. Artemis lunar surface base and Mars transit), astronauts are exposed to unprecedented physiological and psychological stressors:

  • Ionizing Deep-Space Radiation: Chronic Galactic Cosmic Rays (GCR) and acute Solar Particle Events (SPE) causing DNA double-strand breaks and acute radiation risks.
  • Altered Gravity & Hemodynamics: Cephalad fluid redistribution ("puffy-head syndrome"), Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS), and severe orthostatic intolerance upon planetary re-entry.
  • Musculoskeletal Atrophy: Bone Mineral Density (BMD) loss of 1.0% to 1.5% per month in the femoral neck and lumbar spine, combined with postural muscle wasting.
  • Extreme Isolation & Confinement: Circadian rhythm disruption, chronic sleep debt, and cognitive psychomotor vigilance degradation.
The Core Deep-Space Problem: Communication Latency
Unlike the International Space Station (ISS), where flight surgeons on Earth can provide real-time telemedicine, missions beyond low Earth orbit face a 4 to 22-minute one-way communication delay. Astronauts must shoulder the primary responsibility for spotting, diagnosing, and mitigating health anomalies in themselves.

AstroPulse solves this crisis by deploying an on-device Edge AI Medical Officer that runs directly on spacecraft local hardware without requiring internet access. It continuously fuses 8 physiological telemetry streams, computes a real-time Astronaut Health Readiness Score (AHRS), and prescribes actionable clinical countermeasures.

3.2 Explainable AI (XAI) Architecture

Standard "black box" deep learning models are unsafe for aerospace medicine. Astronauts need to know why their risk level is elevated. AstroPulse computes normalized feature contributions (SHAP-style attribution), allowing astronauts to see that e.g. "38% of your elevated strain is driven by radiation accumulation, while 26% is from acute sleep debt."

4. Core Functional Modules & Clinical Protocols

4.1 Command Dashboard & 60 FPS Telemetry Canvas

The central flight cockpit presents an animated, real-time Lead-II ECG waveform canvas simulating heart rate and autonomic variability. The circular Health Readiness Dial displays the astronaut's readiness status (OPTIMAL, MILD STRESS, ELEVATED DECONDITIONING, or CRITICAL MEDICAL ALERT).

4.2 Interactive Psychomotor Vigilance Task (PVT)

Based on NASA's gold-standard ISS cognitive fatigue test. Astronauts trigger a randomized visual stimulus and click as fast as possible. The system logs their real reaction time down to the millisecond, objectively identifying cognitive slowdown and micro-sleeps without relying solely on subjective self-reporting.

4.3 Deep-Space Radiation Dosimeter & SPE Shelter Drill

Monitors Galactic Cosmic Radiation (GCR) background flux versus NASA's 600 mSv lifetime career limit. Includes an interactive Solar Particle Event (SPE) Drill that simulates coronal proton flares, triggers cabin alarms, and guides crew through deployment of hydrogen-rich water wall shielding.

4.4 Cardiovascular & SANS Profiler

Analyzes microgravity cephalad fluid redistribution. Features an Orthostatic Intolerance Landing Predictor that calculates the risk of syncope (fainting) prior to Mars or Earth re-entry, prescribing a pre-landing protocol of 1.0L isotonic saline loading and anti-G compression suits.

4.5 Hands-Free Autonomous Voice AI

Built using edge Speech-to-Text and Speech Synthesis APIs. Enables astronauts in pressurized spacesuits or tethered operations to speak commandshands-free (e.g. "What is my health readiness score?", "Check radiation", "Report blurred vision"). It also evaluates an acoustic vocal biomarker jitter index to detect respiratory and vocal fatigue.

6. NASA Standards Compliance (NASA-STD-3001)

AstroPulse directly enforces standards from NASA-STD-3001, Volume 1 (Crew Health) and Volume 2 (Human Factors, Habitability, and Environmental Health):

  • Standard 3.1.2 (Radiation Health): Enforces the 600 mSv career limit and requires SPE alert storm shelter protocols.
  • Standard 3.2.1 (Musculoskeletal Countermeasures): Tracks the mandatory 2.5 hours/day resistive (ARED) and aerobic (T2) exercise regime.
  • Standard 3.2.2 (Cardiovascular & Orthostatic Conditioning): Mandates fluid-loading protocols and compression garments prior to planetary gravity transitions.
  • Standard 3.3.1 (Behavioral Health & Sleep): Deploys circadian lighting cues (480nm blue-depleted spectrum) and objective PVT reaction monitoring.
  • Standard 4.1.1 (Autonomous Medical Operations): Provides on-board autonomous clinical decision support to mitigate communication blackout risks.
